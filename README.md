## Why "minimum deposit" has four different answers

The confusion isn't an accident. Deposits on an exchange pass through layers, and each layer has its own floor.

The first layer is the blockchain. When you send USDT over TRC20 or BTC over Bitcoin, the exchange isn't setting a minimum — the network is. A satoshi is the smallest divisible unit of Bitcoin, and a deposit of one satoshi is technically a valid deposit. In practice nobody does that, because a withdrawal later would cost more than the amount deposited.

The second layer is the payment rail. Cards, bank transfers and P2P merchants all have their own limits, and those limits are set by the processor or the counterparty. Gate's documentation is clear that fees arising in this process are charged by the payment service provider, not by the platform.

The third layer is your own account status. Identity verification unlocks higher limits and more payment channels; before that, you're working with a smaller set of options. Requirements vary by region and by payment method.

The fourth layer is the exchange's trading rules, which are a different thing from deposit rules. Gate's own educational material describes a $1 entry point on the platform, and third-party reviews put the minimum trade size at $1 as well. That $1 is the number people usually mean when they say "minimum" and it's the smallest amount you can put to work, not the smallest amount you can send in.

## The numbers behind crypto deposits

For crypto, the honest answer is that there's no meaningful minimum. Gate's own explainer on deposit limits states that major exchanges set extremely low deposit thresholds precisely because crypto is divisible — BTC's floor is one satoshi, which is 0.00000001 BTC, and ETH and USDT follow similar logic at the token level.

What does change per coin is confirmation time, and that's where small deposits get annoying. Bitcoin usually needs a handful of confirmations before your balance updates. Ethereum-based tokens can require considerably more, depending on network conditions. Deposits on faster networks like Tron or Solana typically finalise in under a minute. None of this is controlled by the exchange — it's how the chains are designed.

Two practical rules that save people money:

- Always confirm the network before sending. A USDT deposit sent on the wrong chain is not recoverable by support.
- If you're depositing a token you don't recognise, check the confirmation requirement first. Waiting isn't a malfunction; it's the chain doing its job.

## What the minimum doesn't tell you: getting money back out

Almost every article about minimum deposits skips the exit, which is the part that actually bites.

Gate's own explainer on deposit limits is explicit that withdrawal minimums are meaningfully higher than deposit minimums, and that withdrawal fees are driven by network costs rather than a platform markup. That's the standard structure across major exchanges, and it has one practical consequence: a very small deposit can be cheaper to abandon than to withdraw.

So the useful question isn't "what's the minimum deposit" — it's "what's the smallest amount I can deposit, trade, and still withdraw without the fees eating it." If you're testing the platform, something in the $50–100 range is a more sensible starting point than the literal minimum, and Gate's own educational content makes a similar argument for starting small but not microscopic.

## Quick answers

**What's the minimum deposit on Gate?**
There isn't one universal number. Crypto deposits have effectively no floor, Gate's own fiat guide lists $2, and third-party reviews quote $10 for USD/USDT. Your payment channel and region decide which applies.

**Can I deposit just $10?**
Yes — $10 clears most fiat channels and meets the first-deposit task on several versions of the new-user campaign. The catch is on the way out, since withdrawal minimums and network fees are higher than deposit minimums.

**Does Gate charge a deposit fee?**
No. Any transaction cost comes from the payment provider or the blockchain, not the platform.

**What's the smallest trade I can place?**
Around $1, based on Gate's own materials and third-party reviews — but a $1 position is for testing the interface, not for making money.

**Why do different sites give different minimums?**
Because they're describing different funding routes. A card payment, a local bank transfer, a P2P merchant and an on-chain crypto deposit are four separate systems with four separate limits, and regional restrictions change all of them.

**Do I need to verify identity to deposit?**
Verification unlocks higher limits and more payment channels, and campaign rewards generally require it before the deadline. Exact requirements vary by region and payment method.

**How long does a deposit take?**
Card payments usually land within minutes. Bank transfers typically take one to three business days. Crypto depends on confirmations — seconds on Tron or Solana, longer on Bitcoin and Ethereum-based tokens.
